# CoolWheels — Idea Card  
*Reinrollen. Abkühlen. Weiterfahren.*

## 1. Slogan / name
CoolWheels — Public Cooling Station for Wheelchair Users

## 2. For whom (target group)
Primary: wheelchair users and people with mobility disabilities who spend extended
time outdoors, especially during heatwaves.
Secondary: outdoor/delivery workers and other mobility-aid users, as an
after-hours or overflow use case.

## 3. With which problem
- Electric wheelchair batteries and joystick controllers overheat and drain
  faster in direct sun.
- Existing shaded rest points are often physically inaccessible — blocked by
  curbs, no wheelchair clearance, or too far apart along a route.
- Users can't reliably plan routes around where they can cool down, rest, or
  recharge equipment.

## 4. We offer (idea) , expanded
A self-sufficient, solar-powered cooling pod placed along high-traffic
wheelchair routes, offering:

- **Shade + misting** — passive shade structure with a fine-mist cooling fan
- **Wheelchair charging** — insulated outlet for electric wheelchairs and
  mobility aids
- **Solar power (new)** — rooftop solar panels generate the electricity used
  directly by the misting fan and charging outlet, making the pod
  energy-independent and cheaper to operate — no grid connection required for
  most locations
- **Device charging (new)** — a small bank of USB/phone charging ports,
  useful for anyone waiting or resting, not just wheelchair users
- **Long-term micro-workspace option (new)** — for pods sited at
  higher-traffic or longer-dwell locations (e.g. near transit hubs or
  co-working-adjacent areas), an upgraded pod variant includes a small
  fold-down desk surface and Wi-Fi hotspot, letting someone work, study, or
  take a call while their wheelchair charges — turning a purely functional
  rest stop into a place people *choose* to spend time, which also
  increases usage data for the pilot
- **Access control** — entry via an existing municipal accessibility
  credential (e.g. AustriaID / CityGraz), keeping the pod available for the
  people who need it and preventing vandalism/misuse

Two pod tiers:
| Tier | Features | Best placed at |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | Shade, misting, wheelchair charging, solar power | Residential streets, parks, everyday routes |
| **Plus** | Standard + device charging + fold-down desk + Wi-Fi | Transit hubs, town squares, high-dwell locations |

## 5. How does it look
Modular, rounded steel-and-composite shelter (see attached render) with a
curved shading canopy, visible misting vent, side-mounted EV/wheelchair
charge port, rooftop solar array (not visible from street level, integrated
into the canopy), and clear "CoolWheels — Public Cooling Station" signage.

## 6. How could money be earned
- Municipal service/commission contracts, funded through city
  climate-adaptation and resilience budgets
- Solar power lowers long-term operating cost, making the municipal pitch
  stronger (near-zero energy cost after installation)
- Pilot funding via crowdfunding (Kickstarter) or angel investment
- After a successful pilot: minimal insurance-covered user fee, or
  sponsorship (health insurers, mobility-aid manufacturers, local CSR
  programs) on Plus-tier pods with device charging/workspace features
- Plus-tier pods could carry small-format advertising or sponsor branding,
  since they attract longer dwell time

## 7. How radical is the idea
Feasible, leaning left of center on the feasible–radical scale. Every
component (solar panels, misting fans, EV charging, Wi-Fi) is proven,
off-the-shelf technology — the innovation is in combining them specifically
for wheelchair users and gating access through an existing accessibility
credential. Low technical risk, clear pilot path, testable as one unit
before scaling.
