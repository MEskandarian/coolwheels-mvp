## Phase 1 Understand (Group 5)

Category notes: Heat (shade, cooling, water, vegetation), Inclusion (accessibility, equality, safety, comfort), Resilience (adaptability, sustainability, climate-friendly), Public space (streets, squares, parks, walkways), Public health infrastructure (hospital load from heat stroke), Pilot projects (start small).

## Phase 2 Observe
Interview structure from the workbook (introduction, warm-up, deep dive, reflect). Question bank covered older people, people with disabilities, outdoor workers, renters in older buildings without AC, general public space use.

Interviewees and personas (anonymised in the board):
- Lucas: electric wheelchair user, relies on public transport to get to work.
- A woman about 65 with a leg and lower-body mobility disability, uses a wheelchair or mobility aid.
- A man over 50 experiencing homelessness in Graz, around Graz Hauptbahnhof, chronic leg and lower-back mobility impairment.
- A road maintenance worker (Julia in the board): wants to know where she can rest, cannot plan her day.

## Phase 3 Definition: insights
Wheelchair user (woman, about 65):
- F1. Shade only helps if it is reachable. Existing shaded spots are blocked by high curbs.
- F2. Heat changes behaviour and independence: she changes routes, avoids places, postpones activities.
- F3. Accessibility and heat protection must be designed together.

Man experiencing homelessness:
- F4. Without a private place to recover, public spaces are essential for cooling down.
- F5. Shade, seating and water must be located together.
- F6. Public spaces must work for people with nowhere else to go, without requiring a purchase.

Other:
- F7. Electric wheelchair battery packs drain faster and overheat in direct sun. Joystick controllers become hot to use (Lucas).

Problem statements:
- Older people with mobility disabilities need accessible shade, seating and drinking water in public spaces because extreme heat makes moving, resting and using these spaces independently more difficult.
- People experiencing homelessness with mobility disabilities need free, accessible places to rest and cool down because they spend much of their time outdoors and cannot rely on a private space to escape extreme heat.

## Phase 4 Ideation
Crazy Five (Mohammad):
1. Curb-cut-linked shade pods
2. Real-time cool-route street markers or app
3. Free no-purchase cooling shelters at transit hubs
4. Continuous shaded canopy over bridges and chokepoints
5. Solar-powered rest-and-recharge points

Teammate ideas logged: trees and shade every 50 m, accessible water fountains, misting stations and splash parks, cooling islands, roof and vertical gardens, white roofs, sun-tracking solar canopy, personal shade network on wheelchairs (Arduino or ESP32), cooling bus stops, night-chilled cistern with radiant benches, mobile cooling and rest van, open doors cooling network, climate-adaptive wear challenge, upcycled urban shade, ergonomic thermal-safe seating, shaded cool-down zones, cooling stations for wheelchairs.

Matrix shortlist: Cooling Stations for Wheelchairs, Mobile cooling and rest van, Cooling Islands, Heat-safe wheelchair routes, Personal shade network.

D1: chosen idea is Cooling Stations for Wheelchairs, named CoolWheels.
Reasons: grounded in interview data (F7), targets the group with the most highest-pain votes, pilotable as one pod, credential-gated access fits a municipal commissioning model.
Runner-up: Personal shade network (higher technical novelty, cost moves to the individual, weaker fit for a municipal model).
