# CoolWheels: idea and concept

Name: CoolWheels
Slogan: Reinrollen. Abkühlen. Weiterfahren. (Roll in. Cool down. Roll on.)

## Idea Card
1. Name: CoolWheels
2. For whom: wheelchair users and people with mobility disabilities who spend time outdoors in heat. The municipality is the paying customer.
3. Problem: electric wheelchair batteries and joystick controllers overheat in direct sun. Existing shaded rest spots are often not accessible (curbs, no clearance).
4. Offer: pods with shade, misting fan and insulated wheelchair charging on high-traffic wheelchair routes, unlocked with an existing municipal accessibility credential (ID Austria, CityGraz).
5. Look: open-sided, bus-shelter-sized structure, accessible bench, weatherproof charging outlet, credential reader at the entrance, thermal-barrier roof, solar power.
6. Money: municipal service contracts from climate-adaptation budgets, installation sales, Plus-tier sponsorship, pilot funding by angel investment or crowdfunding. Possible minimal insurance-covered user fee after a successful pilot.
7. Radicalness: feasible side. Known technologies in a new, accessibility-first configuration.

## Tiers
- Standard: shade, misting, wheelchair charging, solar
- Plus: Standard plus device charging, fold-down desk, Wi-Fi

## Implementation plan (from Miro)
1. Identify the most-used wheelchair routes.
2. Contact a fabricator for the pods, keep costs in view and present them to the government for subsidy.
3. Distribute access credentials through the municipality.
4. Run the pilot for 3 months.
5. Evaluate usage, comfort and profitability.

## Forum summary posted for the Project Coach (about 290 words)
CoolWheels: Cooling Stations for Wheelchair Users. "Reinrollen. Abkühlen. Weiterfahren."

Extreme heat disproportionately affects wheelchair users, yet existing cooling infrastructure rarely accounts for their needs. Our interviews showed that electric wheelchair batteries and joystick controllers overheat and drain faster in direct sun, and that existing shaded spots are often physically inaccessible, blocked by high curbs or lacking wheelchair clearance. For people who cannot simply go home and cool down, this turns everyday mobility into a health risk.

CoolWheels addresses this with dedicated, accessible cooling pods placed along high-traffic wheelchair routes. Each pod combines shade, a misting fan system and an insulated charging outlet for electric wheelchairs, built with thermal-barrier materials. Access is restricted through an existing municipal accessibility credential (ID Austria or CityGraz), so the pods stay available for people with disabilities and are protected from misuse.

Implementation plan: (1) identify the most-used wheelchair routes through mapping and community input, (2) contact fabricators to design and install pilot pods at cost-conscious specifications, (3) partner with the municipality to distribute credentials and apply for subsidy, (4) run a 3-month pilot, (5) evaluate usage, comfort and feasibility before deciding on scale-up or a minimal user fee.

Revenue potential includes municipal service contracts funded through city climate-adaptation budgets, and initial pilot funding through crowdfunding or angel investment. If the pilot succeeds, a minimal insurance-covered user fee could sustain the model.

CoolWheels combines proven technologies in a new, accessibility-first configuration to close a gap in heat-resilient public space.
